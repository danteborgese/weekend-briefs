💼 Job search (8)

Lenny Rachitsky: PM ascendant

Sriram Krishnan says product management is making a comeback because someone has to design how personal agents behave.

How it applies: This is your opening line for agent and AI companies. You already scope, brief and supervise a team of bots for your own work, so tell that as a PM story in outreach and interviews, with one concrete bot as the example.

Lenny Rachitsky: Ramp CPO on 3 PM paths

Ramp's CPO sees three long-term PM paths: the technical PM, the tastemaker PM and the GM.

How it applies: Pick one and make your resume headline and LinkedIn say it. With a content background, the tastemaker path is the most believable pitch, and it lines up neatly with PMM roles.

Jay Yang: Head of content is underrated

Jay argues a head of content is rare because it takes taste, operations chops and a deep grasp of marketing at once.

How it applies: Those three traits are a ready-made bridge from content leadership to PMM. Use them as the frame for your "why PMM" answer, with one example for each.

Aakash Gupta: AI PM learning path

An 8-step path to AI PM without a CS degree; he says 60% of recent AI PM hires were non-technical.

How it applies: The gap worth closing is evals and prototyping (steps 3 and 5). Hand-label 20 outputs from one of your own bots, write up what you found, and you have a portfolio piece most candidates don't. His resume and interview guides (step 8) are worth a skim before your next application.

Kipp Bodnar: Prospecting on X with Grok Bot

A playbook: one bot per use case scans 30 days of X posts, returns the people in a spreadsheet and scores them against past customers.

How it applies: Flip it for the job search: a bot that finds NYC PMs and hiring managers posting about open roles or their team's problems, scored by fit. Skip his enrichment and cold-call steps; a warm reply to a real post works better. The same pattern could find Doers sponsors later.

spect: SpaceXAI 53-minute Grok Bot workshop

The GTM team shows how they run a team of bots: role, then tools, skill, feedback and a routine.

How it applies: The meeting prep (06:38) and prospect research (08:01) sections map straight onto interview prep: a bot that builds a one-page brief on the company and interviewer before each call.

Mike P: Chat vs agentic AI explainer

A rough 6-minute screen recording explaining chat vs agents, with resume tips.

How it applies: Proof that an unpolished explainer lands. A 3-minute version of your own is a PMM portfolio piece (positioning plus storytelling) and could double as Doers content.

Katie Kirsch: Open marketing and growth roles

A list of open marketing, growth and creative roles at startups, on cosign.co.

How it applies: Mostly growth and creative, so treat it as one more source, not a strategy. Worth a 10-minute scan for NYC PMM titles.

🎙️ Doers (7)

Jay Yang: Test on short form, then multiply

Test angles and ideas on short form, then multiply the winners into long-form, lead magnets and offers.

How it applies: This is the cleanest growth loop for Doers: post clips of existing episodes as tests, and let the ones that hit decide the next newsletter issue and the topics you pitch to sponsors.

Cody Schneider: AI-run hourly clip channel

A Hormozi clip channel, reportedly at 10M views a month, transcribes the library, cuts clips, stitches an offer at the end and posts every hour.

How it applies: Doers already has the library and Clip Bot does the cutting. The missing pieces are an end card (newsletter signup or sponsor read) and scheduled posting. One clip a day is a more realistic start, and the view claim is unverified.

Matt Palmer: A bot that runs his YouTube channel

A bot writes tags, chapters and brand-voice descriptions from a template using the YouTube Data API and AssemblyAI.

How it applies: Directly useful for Doers Distribution, especially the 15 episodes it's about to backdate to Spotify. Ask it whether it already writes chapters and descriptions; if not, Matt's template is the add-on.

Jay Yang: Use AI for operations so you can focus on content

Use AI to run the business side so your own time goes into the content.

How it applies: A good rule for dividing Doers work: bots own distribution, tagging and backdating, and your hours go into the episodes and the newsletter.

Colin and Samir: Kate Tolo's 11 marketing rules

The person behind the Bryan Johnson brand explains the 11 marketing rules she used to build it.

How it applies: Three rules fit Doers now: start with text first (14:09), know your ticket price (24:31) and if it works, say it again (30:22). Text first points to the newsletter as the testing ground for episode ideas.

Aakash Gupta: Motion graphics with Claude in 6 steps

A recipe for single-file HTML animations exported as sharp GIFs: reference, timed states, time as the only input, colors, self-check, export.

How it applies: A cheap way to give Doers and newsletter posts animated visuals in your brand colors. It also pairs with the @EconomyApp visualization chore: build it once, then animate it.

DaVinci Resolve AI editing: Three posts on AI editing
